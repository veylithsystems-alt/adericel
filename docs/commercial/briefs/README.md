# Commercial briefs

One page per product. Each brief states the target buyer, the problem, the
offer, the price hypothesis, the evidence obtained so far, and the next
milestone.

## The evidence discipline, and why it is in a commercial document

These briefs apply the same rule as the technical documents: **a claim is
labelled by how it was established, and nothing is presented as stronger than
its basis.** Four labels, used throughout:

| Label          | Means                                                                                   |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **MEASURED**   | Counted from rows or runs somebody can go and look at. A path or a command is named     |
| **BUILT**      | The capability exists and is tested, but has never run for a real customer              |
| **TARGET**     | A number we intend to hit. Nothing has tested it                                        |
| **ASSUMPTION** | A belief the business rests on, not yet checked against anybody outside Veylith Systems |
| **UNKNOWN**    | We do not know, and saying so is the answer                                             |

A price in code is an **ASSUMPTION**, not a measurement. A passing test is
**BUILT**, not **MEASURED** value. The distinction is not pedantry: a founder
who confuses the two raises money or signs a contract on the strength of a
number nobody has tested, and this product's entire proposition is that it does
not do that to its customers. It would be incoherent to do it to ourselves.

**Nothing in these briefs is `MEASURED` customer value, because no customer has
used either product.** That is the single most important fact on both pages and
it appears on both pages.

## What is deliberately not here

**VSAP (the Veylith Sovereign AI Platform) has no brief.** It is an internal
Veylith corporate service — a local-model inference platform Adericel may call
through a private gateway — and not a product anybody buys. Writing it a
commercial brief would invent a product line that does not exist, which is the
commercial equivalent of inventing evidence.

**White-label presentation has no brief.** It is `DESIGNED` only, with no
implementation, and the current build contract does not mention it. A brief for
it would describe an intention as an offer.
