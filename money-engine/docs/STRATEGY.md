# The Strategy

Read this once. It is the whole reasoning behind the system. Everything else
is plumbing.

---

## 1. Where money actually comes from

There are only three sources. No fourth exists.

| Source | Autonomous? | Needs selling? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Capital returns** | Yes, genuinely | No | You need capital. Returns scale with it. |
| **Arbitrage** | Briefly | No | Decays to zero. You lose to faster players. |
| **Labour / attention** | No | Yes | Every "passive income" course is this in disguise. |

You ruled out selling. That leaves capital and arbitrage.

Arbitrage is a trap for individuals. If a price gap is exploitable by a script
on a home connection, it has already been exploited by a firm with servers in
the same building as the exchange. Retail algo-trading bots have a negative
expected return after fees. **This system does not contain one, and that is
deliberate.**

So the honest answer is: capital.

---

## 2. The part everyone misses

Capital returns scale with capital. Most people conclude "I don't have capital,
so this doesn't apply to me" and go looking for a bot instead.

That's the mistake. There is a fourth thing that behaves *exactly* like income
but isn't on the list above:

> **Money you are already losing, caught and kept.**

A pound you stop losing is worth more than a pound you earn, because it isn't
taxed. £3,000 of plugged leaks is equivalent to roughly £4,400 of gross salary
for a basic-rate taxpayer, and about £5,000 for a higher-rate one.

And unlike a business, it needs no customers, no content, no updates, and
nobody's permission.

For a UK household with one young child, the leak pile in year one is
realistically **£3,000–£6,000**. That is the machine. That is what we're
building.

## 3. The levers, with real numbers

Ordered by pounds-per-minute-of-your-time. Rates change every April — the
system's job is to check them, not to trust this table forever.

### Tier 1 — One-off claims (biggest money, smallest effort)

| Lever | Worth | Your time | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tax-Free Childcare** | Up to £2,000/yr per child | ~10 min/quarter | Quarterly top-up |
| **Marriage Allowance** | ~£252/yr + up to ~£1,000 backdated 4 yrs | 15 min, once | Once |
| **Child Benefit** (claim even if you repay it) | ~£1,300+/yr | 20 min, once | Once |
| **Workplace pension match** — raising your % to the employer max | Free employer money, often £500–£2,000/yr | 5 min, once | Once |

**The Child Benefit one is the most-missed thing in UK personal finance.** Even
if your income means the High Income Child Benefit Charge claws it back, you
should still *register* the claim — because a Child Benefit claim in the name of
the lower-earning or non-earning parent awards **National Insurance credits**
that count toward their State Pension. Skipping the claim can quietly cost tens
of thousands over a lifetime. Register, and tick "don't pay me" if the charge
applies.

Tax-Free Childcare: the government adds £2 for every £8 you pay in, capped at
£500 per quarter per child. That is an instant, guaranteed **25% return** on
childcare money you were spending anyway. There is no investment on earth that
matches it. The catch is you must re-confirm your details every 3 months or it
switches off — which is precisely the kind of thing a system should remember and
you should not.

### Tier 2 — Recurring leaks (steady money, system-detected)

| Lever | Typical worth | Your time |
|---|---|---|
| **Dead subscriptions** | £300–£1,200/yr | 2 min per cancel |
| **Insurance auto-renewal** (loyalty penalty) | £200–£600/yr | 20 min/yr |
| **Idle cash earning 0%** | 4–5% of the balance | one transfer |
| **Mobile contract still charging post-handset** | £100–£300/yr | 15 min |
| **Cashback on spend you're already making** | £150–£400/yr | zero after setup |

Idle cash is the quiet one. £10,000 sitting in a current account at 0% versus a
decent easy-access saver is roughly **£450/year of pure, risk-free, no-effort
money.** Most households have some version of this and never notice, because
nothing bad appears to be happening.

### Tier 3 — Repeatable offers

| Lever | Worth | Your time |
|---|---|---|
| **Bank switch bonuses** | £100–£200 each, several available at any time | 30 min each |
| **Regular saver accounts** | 6–8% on small monthly deposits | 10 min setup |

Bank switching is genuinely free money and genuinely repeatable. The Current
Account Switch Service moves your direct debits for you. The reason people don't
do it is not difficulty — it's that they never notice the offers exist. That is
a detection problem, and detection is what software is for.

### Tier 4 — Actual capital (the only truly infinite lever)

Once the above is running, the surplus it generates goes somewhere. Automated,
regular investment into low-cost, broadly diversified index funds inside an
ISA is the standard, boring, evidence-backed answer. It requires no monitoring,
no timing, and no skill. **It is the only part of this system that is genuinely,
permanently autonomous** — because it's capital doing the work, not you.

Set it as a standing order on payday and never look at it again. The system's
role here is only to confirm it happened and flag if it didn't.

*Not financial advice — I'm an automation system, not an adviser. Fund and
account choices are yours.*

## 4. What this system deliberately does NOT do

- **No trading bots.** Negative expected value. This is the thing you were
  probably imagining, and it's the one thing that reliably loses money.
- **No crypto yield / staking / DeFi.** The advertised returns are compensation
  for risks that aren't advertised.
- **No dropshipping, POD, content sites, affiliate blogs.** All of these are
  selling, all decay, all need constant feeding. You said no. Correct call.
- **No matched betting.** Mathematically sound, genuinely profitable early, but
  it is *work*, it needs gambling accounts, and the accounts get restricted. Bad
  fit for someone with an 8-month-old and no spare attention.
- **No "sell your bandwidth" apps.** Pennies, and usually against your ISP's terms.
- **It does not move your money.** Initiating payments needs an FCA licence. The
  system reads, detects, and tells you. You click. This is a feature: it means a
  bug can cost you attention, never cash.

## 5. The design rule that makes it survivable

You have combined ADHD, a full-time company build, and a baby. A system that
generates a wall of notifications is a system you will mute in nine days, and a
muted system earns £0.

So the engine is built around one hard constraint:

> **Silence is the default. It speaks once a week. It never gives you more than
> three things. Every item names the pounds and links straight to the button.**

If a lever is worth less than the configured floor (default £50/yr), the system
handles it silently or ignores it. It is not a dashboard. Dashboards are things
you must remember to open. This is a system that interrupts you correctly and
then shuts up.

## 6. Honest expectations

**What "fully autonomous" actually means here:** the *detection, monitoring,
tracking and remembering* is 100% automatic and permanent. The *clicking* is not,
and cannot be, because moving money requires being a regulated institution.

Realistic shape of the first two years:

| | Year 1 | Year 2+ |
|---|---|---|
| One-off claims | £2,000–£4,000 | £0 (already claimed) |
| Recurring leaks plugged | £800–£2,000 | continues, plus new catches |
| Switch/offer money | £200–£600 | £200–£600 |
| Capital returns | small | grows as capital compounds |
| **Your time** | ~6 hours of setup + clicking | **~15 min/month** |

Year one is the big one and it is front-loaded. After that the system's value is
defensive: it stops the leaks re-opening, catches every price rise, and keeps
your cash earning. That defensive job runs forever with no input from you, which
is exactly what you asked for.

The compounding part is real but slow, and slow is fine. You're 40 years from
retirement with a kid who's 40 years from your age now. Slow is the whole point.
